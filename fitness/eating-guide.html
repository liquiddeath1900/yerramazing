ANTI-INFLAMMATORY & GUT HEALTH

SWEATING TRIGGERS TO AVOID
Spicy food, hot drinks, high-sugar meals, and alcohol all trigger worse sweating. Favor room temp or iced drinks. Protein + fat + fiber over simple carbs (reduces insulin spikes that activate sweat glands).

Why these numbers? His calories = ~700-800 under TDEE (~3,000) for steady fat loss while preserving muscle. Research shows obese beginners can handle larger deficits without muscle loss. Going below 2,000 risks energy crashes and gym performance — 2,200 is the sweet spot. Her TDEE = ~2,130 (Mifflin-St Jeor), so 2,350 = +200-300 surplus to fuel muscle growth in the right places — glutes, thighs, core. She's building, not cutting. Both prioritize protein above everything else.

The Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Protein first, always
Every meal starts with protein. If you only have time for one thing, eat the protein. Carbs and fats are supporting actors — protein is the lead.
2. Post-workout shake within 30 min
This is the one meal you can't skip or delay. Your muscles are primed to absorb nutrients right after training. Shake + creatine, every time.
3. Pre-workout = carbs only, keep it light
No fat, no heavy protein before gym. Just quick carbs (fruit, rice cake, honey). Fat slows digestion — you'll feel sluggish. See Nutrition.
4. Water > everything
Him: 128 oz (1 gallon)/day. Her: 80+ oz/day. Dehydrated = weak workouts, bad recovery, worse skin. Carry a bottle everywhere. At 285 lbs the standard 0.5 oz/lb rule lands at ~143 oz — 128 is the practical floor.
5. If you miss a meal, don't double up
Missed your afternoon meal? Just eat normal at the next one. Doubling up = bloating + wasted calories. Stay consistent, not perfect.
6. No caffeine after 2 PM (his side)
He trains 8:30 PM — any stim past 2 PM will still be in his system at bedtime (caffeine half-life ~5 hrs). Sleep drives recovery, cortisol, and skin retraction — the pillars the rest of this plan defends. Pre-workout energy comes from the 7 PM pre-workout carbs, not caffeine.

Rest Day Eating

Same targets every day — training or rest. Total weekly calories + protein are what drive recomp; day-to-day carb shuffling adds complexity without measurable benefit at your training age (Morton 2018 / Helms 2014). Keep the same containers, same snack, same shake target. Still take creatine with any meal. Keep it simple.

Track It

Consistency > perfection. The phased calorie plan (2,200 wk 1-4 → 2,400-2,500 wk 5+) only works if you're actually measuring weekly loss. This is also how you catch the Week 4-5 calorie-bump trigger.
The minimum effective tracking
• Weekly (Sunday AM, fasted): weigh-in + waist measurement (navel level, relaxed abs). Log both.
• Biweekly: progress photos (front / side / back · same lighting · same pose · empty stomach).
• Every session: log the main lift (weight × reps) — that's how you see progressive overload in A/B/C.
• Monthly: scroll back and compare. Numbers don't lie, the scale does short-term.
Run it in the Progress Tracker — separate tabs for him/her, CSV export, trend charts. No tracking = no calibration = no week-5 bump = no skin retraction.
