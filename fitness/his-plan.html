The Mission
Body recomposition · skin-preservation pace · 10-14 months
At 275 lbs and 5'7", estimated 32-37% body fat (down from 280 on 4/28). Target 220-235 lbs at 15-18% BF — that's ~40-55 lbs fat loss while building serious muscle. Loose skin is expected; muscle building + slow loss rate is the #1 strategy to minimize it.

Calorie math: TDEE at 275 / 5'7" / 28 / activity 1.55 (Mifflin-St Jeor) = ~3,330 kcal/day. Target 2,500 kcal = ~830 kcal deficit, currently tracking ~2.7 lb/wk actual loss. Top end of Helms 2014's 0.5-1% BW/wk range for natural lifters minimizing muscle and skin loss. Do not drop below 2,500 — 500 cal/day deficit cap (Murphy & Koehler 2022).

• 2,500 splits: 230g protein (1.8 g/kg, Longland 2016) / 235g carbs / 70g fat.
• Loss runs >2 lb/wk × 2 weeks: add 100-150 kcal (½ cup more rice or extra rice cake).
• Loss stalls <0.5 lb/wk × 2 weeks: drop 100-150 kcal. Never below 2,200.
• Recalc protein every 10 lb lost: 270 lb = 220g, 260 lb = 213g (always 1.8 g/kg current BW).

Why full body, three days.

Each muscle ≥2× per week beats 1× per week for hypertrophy (Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016 meta, Sports Medicine). Full body 3× nails that floor for everything. Less soreness per session, better recovery, more muscle groups burning calories per workout.

Warm-Up (Every Session)

WARM-UP — ~15 MIN
Spine-protective warm-up for a hinge/squat program at 275 lbs
Why this routine: disk history + bad knees + heavy loading (squat, RDL, deadlift) = core bracing, glute activation, and knee prep aren't optional. Skip these and your lumbar/knees take loads your glutes/quads should. That's how disks re-herniate and tendons flare.

1. General warm-up — 10 min
Stairmaster, slow steady pace (level 4-6). Goal: break a light sweat, raise core temp, wake up legs. Not a workout — don't gas yourself before you lift.
Note on "slow burns fat, fast burns muscle": that's the fat-burning zone myth. Slow cardio isn't magic — it works here because it's sustainable, low-impact on your back + knees, and you'll actually do it. Fat loss comes from the calorie deficit + lifting stimulus, not from the cardio pace.

2a. Core + glute activation — every session (disk insurance)
• Cat-cow — 8 slow reps Watch →
• Dead bug — 6 each side, slow + braced Watch →
• Glute bridges — 12 reps, squeeze at top Watch →

2b. Knee prep — every session (knee insurance)
Bad knees + heavy lower-body lifting = patellar tendon and patellofemoral joint need a wake-up call before they're loaded. Skip these and the first working set takes the warm-up hit. Research-backed for patellofemoral pain (Collins 2018 BJSM consensus, Rabelo 2018 hip/knee strengthening review).
• Terminal knee extensions with light band — 12-15 reps each leg (band around knee, anchored behind, straighten and lock the kneecap) Watch →
• Monster walks with mini-band — 10 steps each direction (band above knees, half-squat, side-step) Watch →
• Slow tempo bodyweight squats — 8 reps, 3-sec down / 1-sec pause / stand Watch →

Optional connective-tissue stack: take your daily 10-15g collagen + 500mg vitamin C 30-60 min before training (Shaw 2017, Am J Clin Nutr — pre-loading collagen + Vit C ~1 hr before exercise increased markers of collagen synthesis). Doesn't replace warm-up, stacks with it.

BACK FLARE PROTOCOL
If anything in the lumbar tweaks — even mildly
Rule: any new lower-back pain, stiffness, or radiating sensation = skip Workout C (trap bar deadlift) entirely until you have one full pain-free week of warm-ups (cat-cow, dead bug, glute bridges) with no flare-up. Then re-enter at 50% working weight, build back over 2-3 sessions.

During the pause: keep Workouts A + B (hack squat, hip thrust, chest-supported row, seated press are all axial-load-friendly). Drop the trap bar but don't drop training — losing the lifting habit is a bigger setback than skipping one lift for a week.

Sharp / radiating / down-the-leg pain = stop training, see a doctor. Don't self-rehab nerve symptoms. Disk history is asymptomatic now and the goal is keeping it that way.

Workout C — Single-Leg + Power

Lower — Full Body Builder
Trap Bar (Hex Bar) Deadlift — 4x5 Watch →
The biomechanically safest hip-hinge for disk history — and just as effective. Camara 2016 (JSCR): trap bar produces equal or higher peak force, glute, quad, and trap output vs conventional, with a significantly shorter spinal moment arm (you stand inside the load instead of in front of it). McGill explicitly recommends trap bar over conventional for anyone with disk pathology. The testosterone-spike CNS effect that the chest strategy section relies on is fully preserved.

Hip-width stance, neutral grip on the handles, brace core, push the floor away, reset each rep.

Current working weight (2026-05-02): 175 lb × 5 (45 lb bar + 65 lb/side: 1×40 + 1×25). "Felt it but not crazy hard." Add 5-10 lb when 5x5 feels easy at rep 5.

If your gym doesn't have a trap bar — Sumo 4x5 or DB RDL, NOT conventional even at low weight.
Conventional deadlift's spinal moment arm (lever distance from bar to your spine) is the same whether the bar holds 95 lb or 405 lb. Light weight reduces absolute load but not leverage. With your disk history, the geometry is the problem — not the load.

Sumo progression (LA Fitness backup, ~80% of trap bar to start):
• Week 1: 135 × 5, 4 working sets (45 lb bar + 1×45 plate per side) — groove the pattern
• Week 2: 145 × 5, 4 sets (+1×5 per side)
• Week 3: 155 × 5, 4 sets (+1×10 per side)
• Week 4: 165 × 5, 4 sets
• Week 5: 175 × 5 — matches current trap bar working weight. Hold 2 weeks, then bump 5-10 lb.
• Form non-negotiables: feet wide outside hips · toes 30-45° out · bar touching shins · brace HARD before lift · knees track over toes · pull hips through at top, no hyperextension · reset between every rep.

DB RDL backup (if no platform / barbell free): 4x8, heaviest DBs you can grip-hold (70-100 lb each). Equivalent glute/hamstring work, near-zero spinal compression vs sumo.

Trap bar at LA Fitness — ask before assuming. Front desk: "Do you have a hex bar, trap bar, or shrug bar?" Sometimes stored under the deadlift platform or in the powerlifting corner.

Level up (when ready): Conventional Deadlift 4x5 only after 6-12 months pain-free with trap bar (or sumo) work.

Targeting Visceral Fat

Research-Backed — Why Cardio Matters at 275
Belly fat isn't just under the skin. Visceral fat wraps around your organs (liver, stomach, pancreas) and drives insulin resistance, fatty liver, heart disease, and inflammation. At 275 and 5'7", reducing visceral fat is priority #1 for health — and it responds to specific strategies.

The Exercise Edge

Exercise beats diet for visceral fat specifically. A meta-analysis of 117 studies (Verheggen et al., 2016) found diet produces more total weight loss, but exercise is superior for visceral fat reduction — even without weight loss, exercise dropped visceral fat 6.1% vs 1.1% for diet alone. The scale can stay the same while your belly fat drops significantly.
What Works Best
A systematic review of 84 RCTs (Chen et al., 2024) found vigorous aerobic exercise and HIIT are the most effective for visceral fat reduction. Resistance training is still essential for muscle, but for burning belly fat fast — dial up the cardio.

The protocol that works: 2-3 sessions per week, 45+ minutes each, at moderate-to-vigorous intensity (heart rate at 70-80% max — you can talk in full sentences but sound winded).

Dose-dependent: A 2023 BJSM study (Recchia et al.) showed more exercise = proportionally more visceral fat loss. This dose-response effect doesn't exist with caloric restriction — there's a limit to how much cutting helps, but more movement keeps paying off.
Your Best Options at 275
• Incline treadmill walks — already in your cardio protocol, 15-20 min post-lifting
• Pool sessions — zero joint impact, burns 400-700 cal/hour, hits the moderate intensity zone
• Bike or rower HIIT — 20-30 sec hard / 60-90 sec easy, 15-20 min total (NO running at your weight)
• Cycling — older small study (Mourier et al., 1997, n=24, 2 months) showed substantial VAT reduction on 2x45min/week moderate cycling. Treat as directional support for the bigger meta-analyses above, not a target percentage.

Bottom line: The best cardio is the one you'll actually do consistently. A walk you enjoy 4x/week beats a perfect HIIT protocol you dread and skip.

Foods That Target Visceral Fat

These aren't magic — they work alongside your deficit. But research shows specific compounds accelerate visceral fat loss beyond what calorie cutting alone does.
Carotenoid-Rich Foods
Carotenoids are antioxidants that reduce inflammation and can activate fat-burning genes while quieting fat-storage genes. An 8-week Japanese RCT (Takagi et al., 2020) showed carotenoid-rich vegetables reduced visceral fat across all groups.

Add to your meals: Carrots, sweet potatoes, bell peppers, spinach, squash, tomatoes — any colorful vegetable. These also fit perfectly into your meal prep rotation.
Green Tea Catechins
Catechins boost fat oxidation, increase metabolism, and block dietary fat absorption. A 12-week Japanese RCT (Nagao et al., 2007) showed ~600mg catechins/day significantly reduced visceral fat.

How to get it: 3-4 cups of green tea daily, or 1-2 cups of matcha (highest catechin concentration). The caffeine works synergistically with the catechins. Swap one of your daily drinks for green tea.

Skin Management Strategy

The Real Talk — Research-Backed
At 275, significant loose skin is expected. But the research is clear: slow loss rate + muscle building + targeted nutrition gives skin the best chance to retract.

Faster loss = skin can't remodel fast enough. The 700-800 cal deficit hits this rate at your weight.

Chest Strategy — The Man Boobs Plan

Upper Chest Priority — Research-Backed
Real talk: At 275, you're carrying significant chest fat. Chest exercises don't burn chest fat — you can't spot reduce. But the right chest strategy completely changes how your chest looks, even before all the fat is gone.

The Aromatase Problem

Fat Creates a Hormonal Loop
Fat tissue contains an enzyme called aromatase that converts testosterone into estrogen. More body fat = lower testosterone + higher estrogen. Higher estrogen tells your body to store more fat in the chest. It's a self-reinforcing cycle.

How to break it: Heavy compound lifts (deadlifts, squats, rows) acutely spike testosterone. Combined with fat loss from your calorie deficit, this gradually reverses the loop. Every pound of fat you lose = less aromatase = more testosterone stays testosterone.

Upper Chest = The Fix

Build Up, Not Out
The mistake: Focusing on lower chest (decline press, deep dips) pushes existing chest fat forward — makes it look heavier and more prominent.

The fix: Prioritize upper chest. When the muscle along the collarbone develops, it creates a natural lifting effect — pulls everything upward and tighter. The chest looks flatter even before fat is fully gone.

Daily Core Tightening — Stomach Vacuums

Add This Daily — 5 Minutes
Vacuum Hold — Transverse Abdominis Activation Watch →
The transverse abdominis is a deep muscle that wraps around your waist like a natural belt. Vacuums and hypopressive breathing are two of the most direct ways to train it — it's also worked lightly by planks and dead bugs, but not in isolation.

What research actually shows: Preliminary evidence only — Bellido-Fernández et al. 2018 was n=11 female rugby players, 8 weeks, no control group. They saw waist-circumference + TVA-contraction improvements without body-fat change. Sample is too small (and female-specific) to call this proven. Treat vacuums as a low-cost posture/tone play that complements the deficit, not a validated intervention. The bodybuilding tradition of this practice is the stronger argument than the trial data.

How to do it:
• Stand tall. Inhale, then exhale completely.
• Draw your navel inward and up under your ribs — a hollowing motion, not a crunch.
• Hold 15-20 seconds while breathing lightly — don't hold your breath (avoid the Valsalva maneuver, which spikes blood pressure).
• Relax. Repeat 5 times.

Do this every morning. Over weeks, expect better posture, a more controlled midsection, and a slightly narrower resting waist. Bodybuilders have used it for decades for the same reason.

Erectile function specifically

At high body fat, ED is mostly vascular — not low T
Endothelial dysfunction from insulin resistance, high triglycerides, and inflammation. Compounded by aromatization (fat → estrogen). Fat loss simultaneously fixes both. Esposito 2004 RCT: 30% of obese men with ED restored function with weight loss + exercise alone, no meds.

Practical: don't chase T levels until you've spent 4-6 months on the deficit + lifting + sleep. The fat loss may resolve it. If it persists after meaningful loss, see a doctor — could be hormonal, vascular, psychological, or a medication side effect (SSRIs, BP meds, finasteride, opioids).

Gynecomastia — pseudo vs true (self-check, no doctor needed)

Pinch the tissue directly behind the nipple
Pseudo-gyno (just chest fat): soft, fatty, blends into the rest of the chest. Spreads when you pinch it. Fixed by fat loss + the upper-chest plan above. This is what most heavy guys have.

True gynecomastia (glandular): a firm, rubbery disc directly behind the nipple — usually 2-5cm wide, can be tender. Distinct from surrounding fat. Cannot be lost via diet/exercise. Surgical excision is the only proven fix once established. Aromatase inhibitors (anastrozole) are prescription-only and not for self-experimentation.

Self-check: pinch directly behind the nipple between thumb and forefinger. If you feel a distinct firm disc that's clearly different from surrounding fat → likely true gyno. Not urgent unless it grows or hurts — bring it up at the next doctor visit. If it's all uniform soft tissue → pseudo, the plan handles it.

Skip list — none of these work

• Tribulus terrestris — Pokrywka 2014 + multiple RCTs: zero effect on T or performance.
• Fenugreek — mixed at best, most positive studies industry-funded.
• D-Aspartic Acid (DAA) — Melville 2017 RCT in resistance-trained men: actually decreased T at higher doses.
• "Test booster" blends (Nugenix, Test X180, etc.) — proprietary blends, no individual ingredient at clinical dose, no third-party verification.
• OTC aromatase inhibitors — DIM, calcium-d-glucarate marketed as "estrogen blockers": weak evidence, can crash estradiol if they actually work, which is its own problem.
• TRT without bloodwork + endocrinologist sign-off — shuts down natural production, fertility-impacting, lifelong commitment.

Appendix: when you're ready for bloodwork (months/year+ from now)

Ask your PCP (or order through Marek/Quest/LetsGetChecked) for the full panel — not just total T:

• Total testosterone (morning draw, 7-10 AM, fasted)
• Free testosterone (calculated or direct)
• Estradiol — sensitive assay (LC-MS/MS, NOT the standard)
• SHBG (sex hormone binding globulin)
• Prolactin (high prolactin can cause ED + gyno + low T)
• LH and FSH
• Vitamin D 25-OH (target 40-60 ng/mL)
• Fasting glucose + insulin (insulin resistance suppresses SHBG and free T)
• Lipid panel (vascular = erectile health)
• TSH + free T4

Prep: stop biotin-containing supplements 72 hrs before draw (FDA 2017 / Bowen 2019 — biotin skews thyroid + sex-hormone immunoassays). Fast 10-12 hrs. No heavy lift 24-48 hrs prior. No alcohol 48 hrs prior. Two morning draws on separate days is the standard if you get a low result.

Retest cadence: after baseline, retest at 3-6 months once you've dropped meaningful weight (15-20+ lb). Most fat-loss-driven hormonal changes show up in that window. After that, annual is plenty unless you start a new intervention (TRT, anastrozole, etc.).
Bottom line: the fastest path to better T, better erections, and a flatter chest — for you, today — is the plan you're already on (fat loss + lifting + sleep) plus the four supplement adds (D3, magnesium, ashwagandha, UC-II). Bloodwork comes later. Everything else is noise.

Carb Cycling — Honest Take

What the evidence actually shows
Short version: at your training age and bodyweight, carb cycling is mostly placebo. Total weekly calories + protein drive recomp. Day-to-day macro shuffling adds complexity without measurable scale or composition difference (Aragon & Schoenfeld 2017 review of nutrient timing; Helms 2014 ISSN position on physique-athlete macros only shows benefit at single-digit body fat for stage prep). You're nowhere near that.
If you still want to try it — the simplest version that won't break the plan.
Optional simple cycle
Training day (A/B/C/D): +40g carbs, -15g fat. Calories unchanged. Easy way: add ½ cup extra cooked rice to the 4 PM container OR an extra banana to the post-workout shake.
Rest day (Thu/Sun): -40g carbs, +15g fat. Easy way: drop ½ cup rice from the 4 PM meal, add an extra 1 tbsp PB at break-fast.
Net protein/cals stay constant. Only carb-fat ratio shuffles.
The honest test
Try it for 4 weeks. Track weekly weight + waist. If the scale moves the same as the simple plan would have — drop it, the friction isn't worth the placebo. If you genuinely lift heavier on training days and feel cleaner on rest days, keep it. Don't add this on top of an aggressive deficit while also locking in 5 meals/day, supplement timing, and the new training split — you'll burn out tracking. Earn it after Week 4.
What actually moves the needle: hitting 230g protein every day, hitting 2,500 cal ±100, lifting heavy 4x/week, sleeping 7+ hours. Carb cycling is a tier-3 lever — only pull it when tier-1 and tier-2 are dialed.

Common Mistakes Big Guys Make

Mistake #1
Too Much Cardio, Not Enough Lifting
Cardio burns calories but doesn't build muscle. Lifting is the main course, cardio is the side dish.
Mistake #2
Cutting Calories Too Aggressively
Sustained sub-25%-of-maintenance deficits suppress thyroid hormone and free testosterone (Trexler 2014, "Metabolic adaptation to weight loss"). At 275 lb (TDEE ~3,330) that's roughly sub-2,500 long-term — which is exactly where the 500 cal/day deficit cap (Murphy & Koehler 2022) locks the floor. Short cuts of 2,000-2,200 for 2-3 weeks aren't catastrophic — duration + magnitude is what hurts you, not crossing one magic number. Your 2,500 target sits right at the safety line; do not dip below.
Mistake #3
Not Tracking Progressive Overload
Write down every set, every rep, every weight. If last week was 135x6, this week should be 135x7 or 140x6.
Mistake #4
Avoiding Heavy Compound Lifts
Squats, deadlifts, bench, and rows build the most muscle per minute. Compound lifts release more testosterone and growth hormone.
Mistake #5
Scale Obsession
The scale lies during recomp. Track: waist measurement (weekly), progress photos (biweekly), strength numbers (every session).
