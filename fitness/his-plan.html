The Mission

Body recomposition, not just weight loss. At 290 lbs and 5'7", you're estimated at 35-40% body fat. The target: 220-235 lbs at 15-18% BF. That's losing ~50-65 lbs of fat while building serious muscle. Realistic timeline: 10-14 months. Loose skin is expected — muscle building + slow loss rate is the #1 strategy to minimize it.
Phased calorie plan (honest math). Your actual TDEE at 290 lbs with lifting + 10k steps + incline walks is ~3,400, not 3,000 — which means 2,200 kcal/day is a ~1,200 kcal deficit, not 700-800. That's ~2-2.5 lb/week, which is faster than the "slow loss for skin" target.

• Weeks 1-4: 2,200 kcal/day. Expect 2-2.5 lb/week loss (early weight comes off faster — water, glycogen, then fat).
• Weeks 5+: Once weekly loss runs >2 lb for two consecutive weeks, bump to 2,400-2,500 kcal/day. This drops the rate to ~1.5 lb/week — the skin-preservation pace.
• How to bump: Add a second 2 PM protein snack + 1 extra scoop Vega, or bump container rice portions by ½ cup.
• What doesn't change at week 5+: Workout A/B/C, HIIT, supplements, prep, water, sleep. Only calories. Eat a little more across the same slots — same system.

Warm-Up (Every Session)

WARM-UP — ~15 MIN
Spine-protective warm-up for a hinge/squat program at 290 lbs
Why this routine: disk history + heavy loading (squat, RDL, deadlift) = core bracing and glute activation aren't optional. Skip these and your lumbar takes the load your glutes should. That's how disks re-herniate.

1. General warm-up — 10 min
Stairmaster, slow steady pace (level 4-6). Goal: break a light sweat, raise core temp, wake up legs. Not a workout — don't gas yourself before you lift.
Note on "slow burns fat, fast burns muscle": that's the fat-burning zone myth. Slow cardio isn't magic — it works here because it's sustainable, low-impact on your back + knees, and you'll actually do it. Fat loss comes from the calorie deficit + lifting stimulus, not from the cardio pace.

2. Core + glute activation — every session (disk insurance)

Targeting Visceral Fat

Research-Backed — Why Cardio Matters at 290
Belly fat isn't just under the skin. Visceral fat wraps around your organs (liver, stomach, pancreas) and drives insulin resistance, fatty liver, heart disease, and inflammation. At 290 and 5'7", reducing visceral fat is priority #1 for health — and it responds to specific strategies.

The Exercise Edge

Exercise beats diet for visceral fat specifically. A meta-analysis of 117 studies (Verheggen et al., 2016) found diet produces more total weight loss, but exercise is superior for visceral fat reduction — even without weight loss, exercise dropped visceral fat 6.1% vs 1.1% for diet alone. The scale can stay the same while your belly fat drops significantly.
What Works Best
A systematic review of 84 RCTs (Chen et al., 2024) found vigorous aerobic exercise and HIIT are the most effective for visceral fat reduction. Resistance training is still essential for muscle, but for burning belly fat fast — dial up the cardio.

The protocol that works: 2-3 sessions per week, 45+ minutes each, at moderate-to-vigorous intensity (heart rate at 70-80% max — you can talk in full sentences but sound winded).

Dose-dependent: A 2023 BJSM study (Recchia et al.) showed more exercise = proportionally more visceral fat loss. This dose-response effect doesn't exist with caloric restriction — there's a limit to how much cutting helps, but more movement keeps paying off.
Your Best Options at 290
• Incline treadmill walks — already in your cardio protocol, 15-20 min post-lifting
• Pool sessions — zero joint impact, burns 400-700 cal/hour, hits the moderate intensity zone
• Bike or rower HIIT — 20-30 sec hard / 60-90 sec easy, 15-20 min total (NO running at your weight)
• Cycling — study showed just 2x45min/week at moderate pace reduced visceral fat 48% in 2 months (Mourier et al., 1997)

Bottom line: The best cardio is the one you'll actually do consistently. A walk you enjoy 4x/week beats a perfect HIIT protocol you dread and skip.

Foods That Target Visceral Fat

These aren't magic — they work alongside your deficit. But research shows specific compounds accelerate visceral fat loss beyond what calorie cutting alone does.
Carotenoid-Rich Foods
Carotenoids are antioxidants that reduce inflammation and can activate fat-burning genes while quieting fat-storage genes. An 8-week Japanese RCT (Takagi et al., 2020) showed carotenoid-rich vegetables reduced visceral fat across all groups.

Add to your meals: Carrots, sweet potatoes, bell peppers, spinach, squash, tomatoes — any colorful vegetable. These also fit perfectly into your meal prep rotation.
Green Tea Catechins
Catechins boost fat oxidation, increase metabolism, and block dietary fat absorption. A 12-week Japanese RCT (Nagao et al., 2007) showed ~600mg catechins/day significantly reduced visceral fat.

How to get it: 3-4 cups of green tea daily, or 1-2 cups of matcha (highest catechin concentration). The caffeine works synergistically with the catechins. Swap one of your daily drinks for green tea.

Skin Management Strategy

The Real Talk — Research-Backed
At 290, significant loose skin is expected. But the research is clear: slow loss rate + muscle building + targeted nutrition gives skin the best chance to retract.

1. Slow Loss Rate — 1.5 lbs/Week

Faster loss = skin can't remodel fast enough. The 700-800 cal deficit hits this rate at your weight.

2. Muscle Building — Your #1 Weapon

Muscle fills the space that fat leaves. Every pound of muscle = less "empty" loose skin.

3. Skin Health Supplements

  • Collagen peptides: 10-15g/day — improved skin elasticity after 8+ weeks
  • Vitamin C: 500-1000mg/day — required for collagen synthesis
  • Zinc: 15-30mg/day — skin cell turnover and repair
  • Omega-3s: 2-3g EPA/DHA daily — supports skin barrier

4. Hydration

1 gallon (128 oz) per day minimum. Skin elasticity is directly linked to hydration status.

5. Surgery Timeline — Be Patient

Most surgeons recommend maintaining goal weight for 12-24 months before assessing. Skin continues to retract for up to 2 years.

  • Lower abdomen: Very likely needs surgery. Least elastic recoil.
  • Chest: Depends on muscle built underneath.
  • Arms & thighs: May tighten naturally with enough muscle mass and time.

Chest Strategy — The Man Boobs Plan

Upper Chest Priority — Research-Backed
Real talk: At 290, you're carrying significant chest fat. Chest exercises don't burn chest fat — you can't spot reduce. But the right chest strategy completely changes how your chest looks, even before all the fat is gone.

The Aromatase Problem

Fat Creates a Hormonal Loop
Fat tissue contains an enzyme called aromatase that converts testosterone into estrogen. More body fat = lower testosterone + higher estrogen. Higher estrogen tells your body to store more fat in the chest. It's a self-reinforcing cycle.

How to break it: Heavy compound lifts (deadlifts, squats, rows) acutely spike testosterone. Combined with fat loss from your calorie deficit, this gradually reverses the loop. Every pound of fat you lose = less aromatase = more testosterone stays testosterone.

Upper Chest = The Fix

Build Up, Not Out
The mistake: Focusing on lower chest (decline press, deep dips) pushes existing chest fat forward — makes it look heavier and more prominent.

The fix: Prioritize upper chest. When the muscle along the collarbone develops, it creates a natural lifting effect — pulls everything upward and tighter. The chest looks flatter even before fat is fully gone.

Your plan already does this:
• 30° Incline DB Press (Workout C) — EMG studies confirm 30° beats 45° for upper chest Watch →
• Low-to-High Cable Crossovers (Workout A) — matches the diagonal fiber direction of upper chest Watch →
• Face Pulls (every session) — fixes rounded shoulders that make chest sag forward Watch →
• Conventional Deadlifts (Workout C) — the testosterone spike that attacks aromatase at the root Watch →

Daily Core Tightening — Stomach Vacuums

Add This Daily — 5 Minutes
Vacuum Hold — Transverse Abdominis Activation Watch →
The transverse abdominis is a deep muscle that wraps around your waist like a natural belt. Vacuums and hypopressive breathing are two of the most direct ways to train it — it's also worked lightly by planks and dead bugs, but not in isolation.

What research actually shows: A small 8-week study on hypopressive training (Bellido-Fernández et al. 2018, n=11 female rugby players) reduced resting waist circumference and improved TVA contraction without changes in body fat. Small sample + no control group, so don't oversell it — but it's evidence that improved TVA tone can tighten the midsection independently of fat loss. This isn't a fat-burner; it's a posture/tone play that complements the deficit.

How to do it:
• Stand tall. Inhale, then exhale completely.
• Draw your navel inward and up under your ribs — a hollowing motion, not a crunch.
• Hold 15-20 seconds while breathing lightly — don't hold your breath (avoid the Valsalva maneuver, which spikes blood pressure).
• Relax. Repeat 5 times.

Do this every morning. Over weeks, expect better posture, a more controlled midsection, and a slightly narrower resting waist. Bodybuilders have used it for decades for the same reason.

Chest + Loose Skin Timeline

The skin management strategy above (collagen, vitamin C, hydration, slow loss rate) all apply here. Chest skin has better recoil than lower abdomen — muscle mass underneath is the deciding factor.

Common Mistakes Big Guys Make

Mistake #1
Too Much Cardio, Not Enough Lifting
Cardio burns calories but doesn't build muscle. Lifting is the main course, cardio is the side dish.
Mistake #2
Cutting Calories Too Aggressively
Going below 2,000 at your size tanks metabolism and kills testosterone. 700-800 cal deficit is aggressive enough.
Mistake #3
Not Tracking Progressive Overload
Write down every set, every rep, every weight. If last week was 135x6, this week should be 135x7 or 140x6.
Mistake #4
Avoiding Heavy Compound Lifts
Squats, deadlifts, bench, and rows build the most muscle per minute. Compound lifts release more testosterone and growth hormone.
Mistake #5
Scale Obsession
The scale lies during recomp. Track: waist measurement (weekly), progress photos (biweekly), strength numbers (every session).
